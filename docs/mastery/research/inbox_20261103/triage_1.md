## Six recommended teaching increments

**1. Predict before selecting: INB017, foundations → 0.6 → 10.1.** Lones distinguishes grouped/representative evaluation, validation-based selection and repeated test reuse in [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.02497v5). Proposal: first derive the probability of at least one false positive across $m$ independent null tests, $1-(1-\alpha)^m$, then contrast it with Bonferroni's union-bound guarantee, which does not require independence. Students implement a finite simulation with identity-group leakage and many candidate layers, predict which apparent winner survives, and see selected DEV performance diverge from an untouched holdout. Compare raw $p$, Bonferroni and Holm on the **entire searched family**. Fit scaling/PCA only on TRAIN; keep t-SNE/UMAP displays separate from original-space metrics. An immediate test checks that every identity family belongs to exactly one split. Play: vary family size, group correlation and search count. Signature: a convincing result collapses under the correct split/null. This CPU illustration is proposed, not executed; it does not retroactively validate any course result.

**2. A stable loss can have a broken derivative: INB031, foundations → 17.1.** The [v3 paper §2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31642v3) explains two finite-precision failures, including rounding a winning-class gradient to zero while other class gradients remain positive. Proposal: for a single hard-target cross-entropy loss, derive $\nabla_z\mathrm{CE}=p-y$ and $\sum_i g_i=0$ from invariance to adding a common logit offset. For a binary margin $m$, compare $\mathrm{softplus}(-m)$ and the winning-class derivative $-e^{-m}/(1+e^{-m})$ against the subtractive formula. Students implement this small mathematical kernel, predict failure margins by dtype, and plot derivative and conservation error against a high-precision reference. Include equal teacher/student logits, near-equality, ties, offsets and underflow as separate tests; a correct implementation can still reach exponent underflow. Play: change dtype/margin, then predict whether the failure affects forward values, gradients or both. A later controlled grokking rerun would be a distinct authorized study; this review neither patches libraries nor asserts every current version is affected.

**3. Shared space need not mean overlapping clouds: INB026, 12.1 → 11.1/PRH.** The [primary paper §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.02053v2) investigates encoder initialization, nonlinear depth and contrastive optimization as contributors to modality separation. Proposal: build a visible cone-effect illustration with two randomly initialized networks receiving the same input distribution, alongside a linear-network control. Students compute normalized within/across-group cosine histograms and a gap vector; then compare a proposed gap shift or temperature sweep against paired retrieval and class-wise performance. Derive why centering changes cosine geometry and can change the measured gap even when a projection looks improved. Predict whether a smaller centroid gap guarantees better retrieval; include shuffled pairings and equal-norm random shifts to make the answer falsifiable. Signature: similarity histograms and retrieval matrices beside PCA, with optional UMAP clearly labeled as a view rather than proof. Genuine CLIP activations require a later bounded study; the CPU construction is not a replication of the trained multimodal result.

**4. Deletion measures an adapting system: INB016 + INB024, 8.1 → 8.4, crosslink 7.1.** [Hydra's direct/total effect comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15771v1) and [self-repair's normalization analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15390v2) belong together. Proposal: students first build a tiny residual-vector example and derive how changing the final normalization scale changes the correct-token logit even with downstream outputs frozen. Next implement separate measurements for clean fixed-scale direct effect, total resample-ablation effect, scale-only repair and recomputed downstream repair. Predict which ablation can improve a target logit despite removing a positive contributor. Signature: direct-versus-total scatter plus a promptwise repair waterfall; keep negative values and over-repair visible rather than hiding them through clipping. Compare zero, mean and resample replacements, freeze normalization as a causal control, and ablate a final-layer head to isolate the normalization mechanism. Play: vary residual alignment and scale. A later small-model study could test transfer, but this proposal does not reproduce Chinchilla or a full pretraining distribution. This changes the interpretation of circuit necessity, not the requirement to measure it.

**5. Geometry can align, and can be ambiguous: INB007, 11.2 → proposed PRH chapter.** The [paper's Algorithms 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411v2) alternate correspondence estimation and orthogonal updates after geometric initialization. Proposal: students derive the fixed-correspondence Procrustes SVD solution, implement it, and recover a hidden orthogonal map on an asymmetric finite cloud. Then remove pairing and alternate transport with map updates; compare with the paired oracle, identity map, random orthogonal map and symmetric clouds having several equally good assignments. Predict when a low geometric loss will still return the wrong semantic correspondence. Signature: cross-modal retrieval matrices and accuracy versus paired-anchor count, not a single alignment score. Play: alter cluster frequencies, symmetry and initialization; select these settings on DEV and preserve held-out examples. The proposed CPU illustration isolates identifiability. Any real independently trained encoder replication needs frozen models/data and calibrated metrics; success on this illustration would not prove PRH or globally identical representations.

**6. Class commitment is different from memorizing a point: INB025, 13.1 → 13.2.** The [publisher paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54281-3) analyzes speciation and collapse in diffusion under exact empirical-score/high-dimensional assumptions. Proposal: start with a two-dimensional Gaussian-mixture score students can derive and implement. Plot reverse trajectories colored by eventual component, their class posterior and nearest-training-point distance; ask students to predict when classes separate versus when individual training samples become attractors. Compare population-mixture, empirical-mixture and smoothed-score conditions, varying sample count independently of mixture covariance. A single Gaussian provides a control without a meaningful two-class split; fixed solver steps and several step sizes reveal numerical artifacts. Play: change dimension or regularization, then predict the effect on memorization diagnostics. Signature: trajectories and two distinct time courses. A 2D visualization is a mathematical illustration, not a confirmation of the asymptotic phase-transition theorem or learned-image-model behavior. Later denoising-time patching should use a genuine model and preserve this distinction.
