## 2. Claims that should enter the Map and Spec

**S01 — hypothesis and original evidence.** The proposed convergence concerns how corresponding examples relate within representation spaces; it does not require neuron-by-neuron identity. The paper combines empirical cross-modal alignment trends with an idealized contrastive/PMI account. Its own §6 discusses information loss, narrow tasks, shared research biases and uncertainty over what modest alignment scores establish. Caption-density sensitivity supplies a useful test of information availability. Read §§2.3, 4, 6, App A and App E alongside the [author explanation](https://phillipi.github.io/prh/); the [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07987v5) is the authority for limitations.

**S02 — conditional theorem, empirical claim, limitation.** Theorem 1 (PDF p3; §2.1) assumes a finite world, injective renderings, common symmetric positives/marginals, population minimizers and rotation-unique solutions under a rotation-invariant loss/anti-collapse objective. Predictor is identity; learned/action-conditioned predictors are excluded. Proposition 1 establishes uniqueness for whitened MSE with an eigengap; Theorem 2’s mismatch bound, Eq (3), concerns spectral solutions. Proposition 2 separates rotation existence from identification; unequal-dimension orthonormal-row empirical maps fall outside this theorem. Proposition 3 adds independent nuisance views under further assumptions. Proposition 4 rewrites next-token loss; it does not prove the spectral theorem for arbitrary LLMs. Separately, Fig 1/§4.2 reports 42× fewer samples, and §4.3 compares a 109M text JEPA with evaluated LMs up to 72B on alignment benchmarks. §7 states no experiment exactly meets Theorem 1, SIGReg uniqueness remains assumed, and recaptions/groupings introduce paired information. Source: [v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39302v1), [PDF pp3–6,15,20–23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39302v1).

**S03 — critical empirical reassessment.** Width and searching more layer pairs can inflate raw similarity. Permutation calibration must include the reported aggregation/selection, using the same row permutation across layers; calibrating only the winning layer misses selection bias. The paper finds stronger support for local neighbor relationships than global metric agreement after calibration. This is an empirical refinement within its sampled models/protocol, not a universal impossibility theorem for cross-modal geometry. See [§§4–6, App D](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14486v2).

**S04 — critique of the critique.** A 2×2 comparison separates local/global scale from relational structure/metric distances. Its H0 skeleton overlap compares identities of labeled spanning edges while discarding filtration values; its experiments report relational agreement at both scales. Teach this as a competing diagnosis of what was measured, not a rhetorical vote between Plato and Aristotle. See [§§2.2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27252v1).

**S05 — constructive transfer.** Wasserstein Procrustes with geometric initialization estimates a correspondence and an orthogonal map from disjoint embedding sets. Coarse unpaired alignment can be useful without becoming exact universal correspondence. Compare unpaired, few-pair and paired settings explicitly. See [§3, Eq (1), Alg 1, Fig 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09411v2).

**S06/S07 — theory is scoped.** The [EDLN note](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01098v2) studies a specific linear-network/SGD mechanism and discusses ways its conclusion can break; it is not the same theorem as S02. [LeJEPA §2.1 and §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3) combines predictive agreement with SIGReg and dispenses with an EMA teacher/stop-gradient in that recipe. Do not import EMA into its definition or conflate distributional regularization with semantic correctness.

## 3. Placement and the unifying intuition

Recommendation: give PRH a substantial named **research chapter**, provisionally titled **“The Platonic Representation Hypothesis: What Converges, Why, and When?”**. Do not assign a chapter number in this note. This is an explicit user-requested planning addition, not permission to expand the implemented course before repairing the priority pilots. Keep 7.1, 12.1/12.3, 8.4 ACDC and 9.1 implementation priorities unchanged unless the user separately reprioritizes them.

The teaching intuition is: **what two views share determines which distinctions prediction rewards; an anti-collapse mechanism prevents “everything is the same”; geometry asks whether different observers retain compatible distinctions; intervention asks whether those distinctions do useful causal work.** This is our proposed explanatory spine, not a claim that all named architectures optimize one loss or satisfy one theorem.

## 4. Proposed chapter spine and learner contracts

The following are **proposed exercises and predictions**, not reported experiments. Every core function is visible and editable in the eventual notebook; shared helpers handle only loading, display and bookkeeping. Each exercise gets a hand-computable test, a meaningful wrong-method counterexample, expected outputs, hint/help and solution dropdowns. Show real example identities alongside plots. Reference outputs are guided practice; unseen assessment requires a separately frozen target.

### PRH-L1 — Are two rotated spaces different representations?

Concrete question: two encoders disagree coordinate-by-coordinate; do they disagree about the examples?

- Implement `center_gram`, `linear_cka`, `knn_indices`, `neighbor_overlap`, `orthogonal_procrustes`, `retrieval_at_k` in small stages. Exclude self-neighbors; specify ties and normalization; separate mutual-neighbor conventions from a simplified overlap teaching metric.
- Immediate tests: a known rotation must preserve the Gram matrix; identical example order plus a feature permutation must not behave like shuffled rows; all-identical vectors must trigger collapse/tie handling rather than appear perfect.
- Signature result: paired-example grid, within-space neighbor graphs and before/after retrieval heatmaps, alongside full-space Gram residuals. A 2D projection alone does not satisfy the result.
- Predict first: rotation should preserve kernel/neighbor relations but destroy direct cross-space retrieval until a map is fitted. Prediction is conditional on the constructed organism.
- Controls: shuffled row pairing, random orthogonal map, independent random features, constant embeddings; report effective rank.
- Try It Yourself: change anisotropic scaling or a nonlinear warp and predict which metrics change before recomputing.
- Transfer problem: choose whether CCA, a rotation or neighborhood agreement is justified for a new pair; explain the equivalence class being tested.

### PRH-L2 — Does a larger similarity score mean more shared information?

Concrete question: can width and layer search manufacture apparent convergence?

- Implement `permute_correspondence`, `null_scores`, `aggregate_layer_statistic`, `calibrated_score`. Define the exact score using S03’s recipe, not an arbitrary z-score called “the paper’s metric”.
- Tests: identical row permutation must be reused across every layer pair in one null replicate; selected-layer calibration must differ from calibrating a prespecified layer; repeated captions/videos must be permuted as valid groups, not independent rows.
- Signature result: raw and calibrated alignment versus width and number of searched layer pairs; overlay null distributions and a known-signal condition. Publish the search space and permutation budget next to the figure.
- Predict first: independent-feature raw scores may rise under the constructed width/search manipulations; a correctly constructed null should account for that drift. Known injected correspondence should retain detectable signal only where power is sufficient.
- Controls: no shared signal, known shared latent factor, wrong independent-per-layer permutations, fixed versus selected layer. A null finding at low power is not proof of no relationship.
- Try It Yourself: increase layer search while holding representation information fixed, then choose a search-aware statistic.
- Transfer: preregister a model-capability plot that avoids ranking models using a metric that changes its null baseline with width.

### PRH-L3 — Is convergence local, global, relational, or metric?

Concrete question: can two encoders share which examples are connected while disagreeing on their distances?

- Implement `mst_edges`, `edge_overlap`, a local-neighbor structural comparison, and explicitly defined distance-aware counterparts. Document tie-breaking; distinguish our teaching variants from exact S04 reference parity.
- Tests: feature rotations preserve relations; row-label permutation breaks labeled correspondence; a deliberately chosen nonlinear radial transformation need not preserve all edges, so learners must inspect the actual neighbor/MST changes instead of assuming invariance.
- Signature result: a 2×2 panel crossing local/global with relational/distance-aware agreement, displaying matched example labels and calibration results. Include an edge drawing so the measured structure is understandable.
- Predict first: for a constructed example with fixed edge identities but changed lengths, structural agreement remains while metric agreement changes; if edges also change, the prediction must change accordingly.
- Controls: shuffled correspondence, distance-matched unrelated graph, multiple neighborhood sizes, random features. Tune neighborhood size/transform only on DEV.
- Try It Yourself: find a transformation that preserves the nearest-neighbor graph but changes a global spanning edge, or vice versa; a failed search is retained honestly.
- Transfer: write an evidence statement that distinguishes S03’s local-neighborhood interpretation from S04’s structural-scale diagnosis.

### PRH-L4 — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs

Keep this exact paper title as a substantive section, with a plain-language subtitle: **Which positive pairs make observers agree?**

- Organism proposal: a finite state world with two independently encoded injective observation tables, an explicit symmetric positive-pair matrix and an explicitly chosen state distribution. It is a theorem/mechanism lab, not a native image/text replication.
- Implement `validate_positive_kernel`, `weighted_whiten`, `normalized_pair_operator`, `spectral_embedding`, `weighted_procrustes`, `population_prediction_loss`; prove the change-of-coordinates step in prose, then derive the spectral objective. Inspect the eigengap rather than assuming it.
- Tests: exact marginals and symmetry; weighted centering/covariance; exclude the constant eigenvector; match a brute-force small-world oracle; compare subspaces up to rotation rather than matching eigenvector signs. A repeated boundary eigenvalue must disable the uniqueness claim.
- Signature result: two observation inventories of the same named states, positive-pair graphs, aligned representation plots and a sweep of mismatch versus residual/eigengap. Plot the bound only after transcribing S02 Eq (3) with the correct norms and weights.
- Predict first: matched kernels plus valid uniqueness assumptions should yield matching state geometry; crossing a spectral tie or changing positives can invalidate that prediction. No inference to arbitrary trained networks follows from a successful eigensolver demonstration.
- Controls: collapse without the constraint, mismatched kernels with preserved marginals, nongap/degenerate world, lossy observation collision, deliberately wrong row correspondence.
- Try It Yourself: change one graph edge while preserving the marginal, or remove the eigengap, and predict the effect before solving.
- Transfer: give the shortest counterexample to an omitted assumption; compare this theorem’s assumptions with S06 rather than combining their conclusions.

### PRH-L5 — Which details survive good and bad views?

Concrete question: can strong prediction retain a camera/writing-style nuisance instead of the state we care about?

- Extend the finite organism with independent state and modality-specific nuisance variables. Name the intended state rather than defining “semantic” as whatever aligns.
- Implement `sample_state_and_nuisance`, `make_independent_views`, `make_shared_nuisance_views`, `state_variance_fraction`, `fit_probe_train_only`; then compare an identity-predictor spectral solution with a separate tiny explicit prediction organism only in a future authorized implementation.
- Tests: independent nuisances conditional on state versus copies of one nuisance; shuffled scene identities must break the intended positives; probe targets must not leak through masks or split construction.
- Signature result: state-versus-nuisance explained-variance bars, scene/caption or rendered-state retrieval matrix, and alignment versus nuisance persistence/view mixing. Include loss, covariance spectrum and held-out state readout together.
- Predict first: under the constructed spectral assumptions, retaining a stronger nuisance channel can displace an intended state direction; outcomes outside those assumptions are diagnostic rather than guaranteed.
- Controls: zero embeddings, random features with the same dimension, matched nuisance persistence, shuffled positives, pixel-reconstruction-style baseline where it answers the same task. “Good/bad” labels describe a stated factor-retention objective, not universal quality rankings.
- Try It Yourself: redefine the downstream task so yesterday’s nuisance becomes today’s required signal; explain why dropping it is now harmful.
- Transfer: audit S02’s recaption/grouping and hyperparameter-selection caveats, then design a comparison varying views while holding the underlying texts fixed.

### PRH-L6 — Can shared representations actually transfer behavior?

Concrete question: does alignment let one model’s state change another model’s output in the predicted way?

- Start with exact maze or small constraint-world state, connecting 11.3 and 14.5/14.6. Distinguish `fit_anchor_map`, `heldout_retrieval`, `transport_state_edit`, `apply_counterfactual`, `interchange_accuracy` and `off_target_change`.
- Fit maps and probes on TRAIN; choose rank/regularization on DEV; freeze TEST identities and groupings before reading it. Track scene groups, transformation families and any training/reference exposure separately. A renderer holdout with shared semantic templates is not semantic generalization.
- Signature result: aligned-neighbor plot beside an intervention-effect matrix and a correct-state versus behavior-transfer curve. Include example-level successful and failed counterfactuals.
- Predict first: coordinate changes should be correctable in the exact controlled organism; observational agreement need not identify the right intervention or action dynamics in a learned model.
- Controls: shuffled anchors, same-rank random subspace, norm-matched random edits, null edits, wrong action labels, oracle-state ceiling and an independently trained/held-out world where applicable.
- Try It Yourself: find a pair with a good probe but failed edit, then distinguish map error, off-manifold intervention and genuine non-use of the variable.
- Optional S05 transfer: implement a small unpaired correspondence/Procrustes alternation, compare with paired anchors, and expose symmetric-world non-identifiability and initialization failures. This is an extension, not evidence that first/second moments identify every rotation.
