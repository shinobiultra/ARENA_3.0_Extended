## Unit 6: Generalization (released)

### Title and the questions it asks

Handout title: [Assignment 6: Generalization](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/assignments/a6_generalization.pdf) (homepage tag: "Generalization: Data-efficient algorithms"). Its framing: instead of one pass over a ~600M-token set, train on a fixed 30M-token DNA pool "as many times as we like" and "get the best possible held-out loss using these 30M tokens". The two baseline levers are repeating the data (epochs) and scaling parameters; the problems then test other ways to buy data efficiency.

Four problems (handout pp. 4–7):

1. **Architecture.** (a) Recall: parameter scaling from a supplied tuned dense d4–d10 suite; fit L(N) = L∞ + A·N^−α; is it monotone, do returns diminish, how do epochs and compute vary with size. (b) Recall: the A5 four-expert top-1 MoE vs dense d8, ΔL at equal active compute. (c) Recall: the A5 looped model (one prelude, six passes of a shared core, one coda) vs dense d8. (d) Exploration of data-efficient architectures at a fixed parameter budget.
2. **Regularization.** (a)(i) Recall: the LR × WD grid at a fixed 16 epochs, quadratic fit in log LR and log WD as in A2, and loss against LR×WD; how much larger is the best WD than the old 0.1 reference. (a)(ii) Hyperball vs AdamW with and without WD tuning. (b)(i) Attention-probability dropout (0.05, 0.15, 0.25) on a single model, dropout off at evaluation. (b)(ii) Independent ensembles from five supplied no-dropout models: loss vs ensemble size K = 1…5 averaged over all member subsets, a scaling law L(K) = L∞ + A·K^−α, paired resampling, "five members may not identify the infinite-ensemble limit". (b)(iii) How ensembling and dropout compose.
3. **Optimization.** (a) Linear vs constant LR schedules at several horizons, with sharpness reasoning. (b) Recall: batch size B ∈ {16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096} with LR and WD scaled by √(B/64). (c) SAM vs AdamW with the supplied sharpness probe. (d) Exploration of other optimizers.
4. **Fine-tuning.** Four starting points: related DNA (eukaryotic genic windows from OpenGenome2, excluding target and validation chunks), DCLM text (body transferred, DNA embedding and head re-initialized), nucleotide noise (IID A/C/G/T) and scratch. (a) Compare all four at 1, 4 and 16 passes on the 30k pool, tuning LR. (b) Fine-tuning pools of 1k, 3k, 10k, 30k at 16 passes; one scaling law per initialization; map pretraining benefit to an effective sample count. (c) Adaptations of the related-DNA checkpoint: WD tuning, decay toward the pretrained weights, frozen head, frozen body. (d) Exploration.

Lectures (summary of what they cover, not a copy; slide numbers are PDF pages): [lecture 10](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/slides/lecture_10.pdf), "Regularization and fine-tuning", 116 pp. — data-constrained regime (pp. 6–10), bias–variance, double descent and ridge (pp. 15–24), epoching and parameter scaling at 200M tokens of DCLM (pp. 28–43; the slides say the assignment itself is on DNA, p. 28), ensemble member scaling and seed science (pp. 46–52), dropout (p. 53), composing recipes (pp. 56–60), distillation (pp. 76–85), downstream and OOD (pp. 86–89), fine-tuning, effective samples, forgetting (pp. 91–115). [Lecture 11](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/slides/lecture_11.pdf), "Compression", 104 pp. — information theory, Kolmogorov complexity, Solomonoff induction and MDL (pp. 5–48), description length in architecture: looping, MoE, sequence methods (pp. 49–65), sharpness: batch size, learning rate, SAM (pp. 66–80), data filtering and augmentation (pp. 81–103). The handout cites Kim et al. (2025), Hernandez, Kaplan et al. (2021), Keskar et al. (2017) and Jiang et al. (2020). Two of these are linked from the schedule: [Pre-training under infinite compute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14786) and [Scaling Laws for Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293). The handout's own reference list is not in the PDF text, so I did not verify the other two. The handout also mentions "the DART task (Patel et al., 2024)" for a shuffled-sequence downstream check (handout p. 3); I identified this as [DART-Eval, arXiv 2412.05430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05430) **(inference)**.

### Experiments, models and datasets

- **Model.** d8: eight blocks, width 512, QK normalization, 30,437,888 parameters, MLP intermediate 1792 ([`parameter_scaling.json`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/assignments/blob/7f0db017cb89e31c7d387b907480f977dd033127/experiments/a6_generalization/references/parameter_scaling.json), [`lr_wd_runs.json`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/assignments/blob/7f0db017cb89e31c7d387b907480f977dd033127/experiments/a6_generalization/references/lr_wd_runs.json)). It is the same d8 family as units 1–5. The A6 code imports the **A5** model classes for the MoE and looping variants ([`models.py`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/assignments/blob/7f0db017cb89e31c7d387b907480f977dd033127/experiments/a6_generalization/models.py)), so u06's code depends on u05's.
- **Recipe.** AdamW (0.9, 0.95), LR .003, WD 3.2, batch 64 (micro-batch 8), clipping 1, 32 epochs, 4,800 sequence exposures of warmup then linear decay to zero; "the default baseline is the sweep-selected 32-epoch D8" ([A6 README](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/assignments/blob/7f0db017cb89e31c7d387b907480f977dd033127/experiments/a6_generalization/README.md)). 32 epochs is 983.04M tokens of exposure over 30.72M unique tokens (handout p. 1).
- **Data.** A subset of the Arc Institute's [OpenGenome2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arcinstitute/opengenome2) (the Evo 2 training corpus): metagenomes, eukaryotic genic windows, bacterial and archaeal genomes, mRNA, viral sequences, noncoding RNA, organelles, promoters. Character-level tokens, 19-token vocabulary (A/C/G/T, eleven ambiguity symbols, U, X, BOS, EOS), 1,024-token chunks, 30,000 training chunks (30.72M tokens) and a disjoint 1,000-chunk validation set (handout p. 2). The handout warns that neighbouring or homologous sequences can cross splits, so this tests generalization within the mixture and not to held-out species or genomes. There is **no student test split**: selection and reporting use the same validation set, and the handout says repeated selection "can make validation results optimistic" (handout p. 1). The prepared-data repository is pinned at [`620f5e89`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kothasuhas/opengenome2_tokenized_n12361963_ctx1024_char19_bos_eos_iid/tree/620f5e892cd428813cfb233e54802f43cc8b892a).
- **Metric.** Next-token validation NLL (the supplied files call it `validation_nll`). One downstream check (DART, shuffled-vs-real regulatory sequences) appears only as a handout figure and "does not select recipes" (handout Fig. 1 caption).
- **Seeds.** Reference runs use model seed 42 and data seed 42, except the ensemble bank (seeds 42–46). The handout asks students to confirm main comparisons with a second seed and to pair seeds and data order across methods.

### What a held-out prediction looks like

**The unit 6 quiz is published** (2026-11-22): [interactive page](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/quiz-generalization.html), [PDF](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/quizzes/generalization_prediction_quiz.pdf) (14 pp., created 2026-11-20) and [answer slides](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/quizzes/generalization_answer_slides.pdf) (56 pp., "Quiz 6 - Generalization", dated 11/19). The page and slides were added in [e7a520ae](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/commit/e7a520ae); [1aeb821f](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/commit/1aeb821f) then changed its partial-credit and Q7 grading. The quiz is course material: the notes below describe its structure and link to it, with no answers reproduced.

Shape of the quiz (from the PDF):
- 40 minutes, 100 points, 7 questions, labelled by difficulty. It uses the A6 DNA setting and the default d8 recipe (LR 0.003, WD 3.2, batch 64, 32 epochs, 75 warmup updates then linear decay, no dropout) and compares terminal validation NLL. Code changes are given as diffs against the A6 release (base `7f0db01`, the same commit pinned above); the PDF refers to an `answer_key_code.zip` for the full runnable code, which the quiz page does not link, and I did not find it.
- Q1 architecture: MoE expert count {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8} (pick the matching plot) and Full Attention Residuals vs dense. Q2 stochastic regularization: attention dropout 0.05 for a single model, a ten-model ensemble and inference-time masks; stochastic depth composed with dropout and with a five-model ensemble. Q3 fine-tuning: which initialization prefers the smallest LR at 3k samples; which initialization is best at 30 and 30,000 samples. Q4 LR schedules: constant, 5/10/20/50% warmup-stable-decay and linear, at 16–256 epochs, at a fixed and at a tuned LR. Q5 optimization: batch size 16–4096 under √ scaling (pick the plot) and a ranking of AdamH, Muon and SAM after LR tuning. Q6 EMA, sharpness and generalization: rank Adam-preconditioned sharpness for constant, constant+EMA and linear, and pick the best and worst LR-tuned recipes. Q7: rank six "intervention packages", one per question.
- Answer types are mixed: pick a plot (A–F), a three-way comparison (<, =, > with a stated 0.001-NLL tolerance), pick one cell per row, or rank. Q1–Q6 allow written reasoning that "can recover up to 75% of missed points"; Q7 allows none. Several choices are graded within 0.001 of the best mean.
- The quiz stresses seed noise: it says the estimated run-to-run standard deviation is 0.001 NLL, uses "matched seeds" (three or five) throughout, and the answer slides' opening retrospective says absolute loss differences are smaller and "loss is much higher variance (quiz answers have seed replication)" ([answer slides](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/quizzes/generalization_answer_slides.pdf)).
- The quiz goes beyond the assignment's text: attention residuals, stochastic depth, EMA, Muon, warmup-stable-decay and the packages ranking do not appear among the handout's problems (the handout mentions SAM, Hyperball, dropout and ensembles). This matches the homepage's statement that questions vary in "how much extrapolation they require from the assignment suggestions" ([homepage](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/#schedule)).
- Timing: the quiz page appeared three days after the 19 Nov quiz (22 Nov); earlier quizzes followed the same pattern (Stability 29 Oct → [2 Nov](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/commit/ad25525034e77cabe7ccd5d1a4847c2e03b3e78d), Capacity 10 Nov → [15 Nov](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/commit/fc03c8b5fcba9e80d555db1689d1598b42f3844d)).

What the assignment's supplied reference numbers imply about question shape **(arithmetic on the supplied bundle, rounded; for scoping only, the files are linked, not copied)**:
- Most A6 effects are small in NLL. ### Compute needs

- **Course default:** "a single NVIDIA H100 on Modal" for d8 runs; Modal listed the H100 SXM5 at $3.95/hour on 2 Oct 2026, billed per second ([homepage](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/#schedule)). Course students get a 48 GPU-hour budget per assignment and 2 concurrent GPUs in the course Modal workspace ([repo README](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/assignments/blob/7f0db017cb89e31c7d387b907480f977dd033127/README.md)); these limits apply to students of the course, not to self-study.
- **One d8 baseline:** 30,437,888 parameters, about 15,000 optimizer steps (the manifest records 15,008), 983.04M tokens of exposure, and a published `training_flops` of 2.29×10¹⁷ ([`parameter_scaling.json`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/assignments/blob/7f0db017cb89e31c7d387b907480f977dd033127/experiments/a6_generalization/references/parameter_scaling.json)). **No wall-clock time is published, and none was measured here.** At sustained 50, 100 or 200 TFLOP/s that FLOP count is about 76, 38 or 19 minutes **(arithmetic)**; achieved throughput on a 24 GB laptop GPU or the Julia L40S is unmeasured.

## Unit 7: Exotic, reinforcement learning (released as assignment and code)

### Title, setting and the questions it asks

Handout: [Assignment 7: Exotic — Reinforcement Learning](https://deep-learning-alchemy.github.io/assets/assignments/a7_exotic_rl.pdf) (10 pp., PDF created 2026-11-20), due 3 December 1:00pm ("quiz time"), ungraded, report of at most 20 pages. Code: a **separate repository**, [deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy), at commit [`d523d49d`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy/commit/d523d49daf230435db4fd01d3a0f03851a961465) (2026-11-20 15:17 UTC; the starter commit is [`f2af454a`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy/commit/f2af454acb15062abca6d50b6a71fd3c0c6c34f9), 2026-11-20 01:03 UTC). The handout says it "uses a separate repository, rl-alchemy, rather than the shared course repository". The README describes it as the starter for "policy gradients" on two environments ([README](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy/blob/d523d49daf230435db4fd01d3a0f03851a961465/README.md)).

This is a different organism and a different success measure from units 1–6: the handout opens with "There is no validation loss". Problems 1–3 train a policy for Gymnasium's MuJoCo Humanoid; Problem 4 uses a small gridworld. Two rewards matter: the **proxy** reward the agent trains on (Gymnasium's default: forward velocity weighted 1.25, +5 per step upright, small torque and contact costs) and the **true** reward (forward velocity alone, never used for training), whose episode sum is "forward progress". A standing robot earns about 5,000 proxy return and zero forward progress. Reported metrics are proxy return, forward progress and episode length, at the end of training and averaged over training. The handout asks for at least three seeds for any comparison drawn, spread across seeds, collapsed runs recorded, and a separate learning-rate search (at least three values) for every variant.

Four problems (handout pp. 6–10):
1. **Do the pre-training intuitions transfer?** (a) Batch size B ∈ {8, 32, 128} episodes jointly with LR, at fixed env-step budget and at fixed updates; gradient noise scale over training. (b) Is η·λ the right quantity: weight decay swept jointly with LR, parameter-norm equilibration time 1/(ηλ) vs run length. (c) Width {16, 64, 256, 1024} at depth 1 and depth {1, 2, 4, 8} at width 64, tuned with the starter's `fair()` helper (zero-initialized residual blocks, an adaptive LR holding the per-update KL near 0.02), compared with untuned controls and explained with `kl_update` and `clip_frac`. (d) LR schedules: constant, linear, cosine, cosine with warmup, warmup-stable-decay, each at two peak LRs. (e) Weight averaging: EMA, and interpolation between trained policies from independent seeds and from forks of a shared checkpoint at 1M and 2.5M steps, looking for barriers.
2. **Correcting for off-policy data.** Data from a policy `policy_lag` batches old (ℓ ∈ {0, 4, 16}): (a) no correction, including a derivation of when the expected gradient equals the policy gradient; (b) importance sampling; (c) clipped importance ratios (V-trace style, c ∈ {1, 2, 5}) with a bias derivation; (d) PPO's clipped objective; (e) full PPO (10 passes per batch), ranking all variants at each staleness.
3. **Exploration and the optimization landscape.** (a) The "CRAWL" local optimum: Humanoid with the fall-ends-episode rule removed; render behaviours; interpolate toward a walker and probe random directions. (b) Escape attempts: entropy bonus, larger initial action noise, larger batch or LR, a larger survival bonus. (c) Removing the fall rule from a trained walker, after the full run or after 2M steps. (d) Start-state noise {0.01, 0.05, 0.1} evaluated at those levels and a held-out 0.2.
4. **When and how do agents hack their reward?** "CoinLoop", a 5×5 gridworld (goal 8 steps away, +1 true reward; a proxy coin worth +0.2 that recharges after 4 steps; episodes cut at 50 steps; time not observed by default). (a) Using the exactly optimal proxy policy in two simplified settings (`coinloop_solve`), predict for γ ∈ {0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99} whether REINFORCE farms the coin or finishes (`coinloop_predictions`), then train with and without a time observation. (b) KL-regularised fine-tuning of a briefly trained reference policy on the proxy (c_KL sweep): does true return rise then fall, and do runs with different c_KL fall on one true-return-vs-KL curve.

Handout references are cited by author and year only (for example Schulman et al. 2017, Espeholt et al. 2018, Skalse et al. 2022, Gao et al. 2023); the handout's reference list is not in its PDF text, so I did not verify them. The Exotic lecture on 1 December is unpublished, so there are no lecture slides for this unit.

### Experiments, models and datasets

- **No dataset.** The data are generated by the policy interacting with the environments; there is no fixed corpus, split or validation set.
- **Organism.** Default `PGConfig`: a residual MLP policy (GELU, width 64, one block) with a Gaussian action distribution and learned state-independent log standard deviation; a tanh MLP value network (256–256); AdamW at η = 3×10⁻⁵ with λ = 0; PPO-style updates (GAE λ = 0.95, γ = 0.99, clip 0.2, gradient clip 0.5, 10 passes × 32 minibatches per batch); 32 episodes per batch; 5,000,000 environment steps; evaluation on 8 episodes every 250k steps (handout Table 1, p. 4; [`reinforce.py`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy/blob/d523d49daf230435db4fd01d3a0f03851a961465/reinforce.py)). Humanoid has a 348-dimensional observation and 17 actions, with a 1,000-step episode limit. This is not the d8 transformer of units 1–6.
- **Environments.** [Gymnasium](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/humanoid/) MuJoCo Humanoid (Gymnasium is MIT-licensed and MuJoCo Apache-2.0, per their [Gymnasium](https://api.github.com/repos/Farama-Foundation/Gymnasium) and [MuJoCo](https://api.github.com/repos/google-deepmind/mujoco) repository metadata) and CoinLoop, defined in [`envs.py`](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy/blob/d523d49daf230435db4fd01d3a0f03851a961465/envs.py).
- **Code surface.** REINFORCE and PPO-style trainer, parallel environment stepping, per-update diagnostics (KL, clip fraction, gradient noise scale, effective rank and others), a GIF renderer, weight-averaging and interpolation tools, and Modal launchers ([README](https://github.com/deep-learning-alchemy/rl-alchemy/blob/d523d49daf230435db4fd01d3a0f03851a961465/README.md)). `uv run pytest -q` is documented as about 10 s of CPU checks.
- **Seeds.** Each starter defines its runs with 3 seeds per setting.
